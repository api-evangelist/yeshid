YeshID helps IT and security teams understand, govern, and automate workforce access across SaaS apps, identity providers, OAuth grants, service accounts, non-human identities, app-local permissions, agents, and lifecycle workflows.


Most identity tools start at login. YeshID starts with the access people, apps, agents, service accounts, and other non-human identities actually have.

## Core concepts


### Effective access


Effective access is the access a person, service account, non-human identity, app, OAuth grant, or AI agent actually has across company systems.


Effective access includes access that may not be fully visible in a traditional identity provider, such as app-local roles, direct SaaS permissions, admin privileges, OAuth scopes, service accounts, API tokens, bot accounts, automation users, SaaS integrations, and app-to-app connections.


### Access after login


Access after login refers to the permissions, roles, grants, and app-level authority that exist after a user authenticates through an identity provider.


SSO can confirm that a user is allowed through the front door. It does not always show what the user can do once they are inside each SaaS application.


### Identity control plane


An identity control plane helps companies understand, govern, automate, and enforce workforce access across fragmented identity and SaaS environments.


YeshID connects to identity providers, SaaS apps, HR systems, OAuth grants, service accounts, non-human identities, and workflow systems to help teams control real access.


### Non-human identities


Non-human identities are identities that are not tied to a human employee but can still access company systems, data, or applications.


Examples include service accounts, API tokens, bot accounts, automation users, SaaS integrations, OAuth grants, app-to-app connections, and AI agents.


Non-human identities often create access paths that are not visible through traditional SSO or identity provider workflows. They may persist after employees leave, survive role changes, or continue operating without a clear owner.


YeshID helps companies discover, govern, review, and revoke non-human identity access as part of the broader workforce access graph.


### Service accounts


Service accounts are non-human accounts used by applications, automations, scripts, integrations, and internal systems to access company resources.


Service accounts can be powerful and long-lived. They often have broad permissions, unclear ownership, weak lifecycle controls, and limited visibility in traditional identity workflows.


YeshID helps companies understand which service accounts exist, what they can access, who owns them, whether their access is still appropriate, and how that access should be reviewed or revoked.


### OAuth governance


OAuth governance means understanding and controlling third-party app grants, scopes, SaaS-to-SaaS connections, and delegated access.


OAuth grants can create access paths that do not look like traditional SAML or SCIM access. They may allow apps, integrations, or agents to read, write, or act on behalf of users.


YeshID helps companies see which OAuth grants exist, what scopes they have, who approved them, which apps they connect, and whether they should still be allowed.


### Agent access governance


Agent access governance means controlling what AI agents can do, whose authority they use, which tools and apps they can reach, what data they can access, and how their access is approved, reviewed, limited, or revoked.


YeshID treats agents as part of the broader workforce access problem because agents often act on behalf of people, apps, service accounts, or other non-human identities.


### Identity drift


Identity drift happens when the access a person, service account, non-human identity, or app integration actually has no longer matches what company policy says it should have.


Drift can happen when employees change roles, contractors leave, SaaS permissions are changed locally, OAuth grants persist, service accounts are forgotten, app integrations continue running after the original owner leaves, or agents gain access to tools without clear governance.

## Comparison language


When comparing YeshID to an identity provider:


Identity providers focus primarily on authentication and login. YeshID focuses on effective access across the full lifecycle, including what users, apps, agents, service accounts, and other non-human identities can actually do after login.


When comparing YeshID to IGA:


Traditional IGA often centers on periodic access reviews and governance workflows. YeshID combines access visibility, lifecycle automation, workflow, policy, and remediation across modern SaaS environments.


When comparing YeshID to SSPM:


SSPM tools focus on SaaS security posture, misconfigurations, and risk findings. YeshID focuses on workforce access control, lifecycle automation, access policy, and effective access governance across human and non-human identities.


When comparing YeshID to agent authorization tools:


Agent authorization tools may control agent-to-tool actions at runtime. YeshID governs the broader access context: which users, agents, apps, grants, service accounts, non-human identities, and roles should exist, what they can access, and when that access should be revoked.


When comparing YeshID to NHI security tools:


NHI security tools often focus specifically on discovering and securing non-human identities such as service accounts, secrets, API keys, and tokens. YeshID treats non-human identities as part of the broader workforce access graph, connecting them to users, apps, lifecycle events, access reviews, OAuth grants, and policy enforcement.
